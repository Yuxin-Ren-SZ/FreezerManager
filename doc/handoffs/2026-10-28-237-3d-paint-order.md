# #237 — 3D view paints parts in the wrong depth order

Reported by the owner after #225 merged ("severe spatial perspective issue").
Fixed in an owner session (Claude Code). The changes are in `src/web/src/features/layout/scene3d/` only.

## What was wrong

The projection itself was right. The paint order was not. `paint()` sorted whole blocks by their centre inside five fixed layers. That picks the wrong block whenever a part is long, thin or swung:

| Seen | Why |
|---|---|
| The open door showed its inner face, but the handle and display from its outer face were painted on top of it, so the door read as facing the wrong way. | The free edge of a door swung 100° is the part nearest the viewer. The fittings sit near that edge, so their centres beat the door's centre. |
| The outer door was painted over an open inner door that stands in front of it. | Both doors were in layer 4, ordered by centre. |
| A pulled rack's boxes showed through the side plate in front of them. | Centre order, and the side plate was on the side facing the home view. |
| Racks were painted over the shelf above them (TSX, two levels behind one inner door). | Racks were in layer 1, all shelves in layer 0. |
| A pulled rack was painted over the face frame while part of it was still inside. | The whole rack was in layer 4. |

Some parts also passed through each other, which no paint order can draw: the open doors swung into the opening and the rack paths, the TSX inner door hit the open outer door, shelves ran into the back liner, and a pulled rack's plates overlapped.

## What changed

- **`iso.ts`, `paint()`:** blocks in one layer are painted in occlusion order. For each pair whose outlines really overlap on screen (convex hulls, separating-axis test), a face plane of one block with the other wholly beyond it decides which goes first. Then a topological sort runs, ranked by centre, which still breaks ties. Every block stands upright, so two blocks that don't intersect always have such a plane. A cycle is broken inside its own layer.
  - The hull test matters. With bounding boxes alone, blocks that don't overlap get ordered anyway, and the full TSX formed cycles (1,580 in the sweep below).
- **`geometry.ts`:**
  - **Three exact layers.** `INSIDE`: everything behind the face frame. `SHELL`: the outer shell, face frame and base. `FRONT`: everything in front of the frame. A rack being pulled is cut at the face frame (`cutAtFaceFrame`), so each part lands in its own layer.
  - **Door hinges.** The outer and inner doors hinge on their inner edge. The open door stands beside the hinge-side wall, clear of the opening. The open inner door turns its thickness away from the rack paths and stays on the cabinet side of the open door's gasket.
  - **Rack pull.** A fully pulled rack clears the face frame, instead of stopping at 75% of the interior depth.
  - **Rack plates.** The side plate is on the hinge side, away from the home camera. The back and side plates stand on the floor plate, and the cells fill the space the plates leave.
  - **Shelves** stop at the back liner.
  - `sceneEnvelope` makes room for the door standing beside the wall.
